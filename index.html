Probability of Failure (1–4 pts)

1 · Low
Minor branch/crown dieback; minor defects or wounds.
2 · Mod.
Stem decay/cavity within safe shell limits (>1 in. sound wood per 6 in. stem diameter); crack(s) without extensive decay; defect(s) affecting 30–40% of circumference; crown damage (hardwoods to 50%, pines to 30%); weak union with included bark; girdling roots <40%; root damage <40% within critical root radius (CRR).
3 · High
Stem decay/cavity at or exceeding shell safety limits (<1 in. sound wood per 6 in. diameter); cracks in contact with soil or other defects; defect(s) >40% of circumference; crown damage (hardwoods >50%, pines >30%); weak union with crack/decay; girdling roots >40%; root damage >40% CRR; leaning tree with recent root breakage/soil mounding, crack, or extensive decay; standing dead tree, no other significant defects.
4 · Ext. High
Stem decay/cavity exceeding shell limits with severe crack; stem/branch split in half; defect(s) >40% with extensive decay or crack(s); weak union with crack and decay; leaning tree with recent root breakage and crack/decay; broken hanging dead branches or with a crack; standing dead tree with other defects; visual/physical obstruction of traffic.

Size of Defective Part (1–3 pts)

1
Parts less than 4 inches in diameter.
2
Parts 4 to 20 inches in diameter.
3
Parts greater than 20 inches in diameter.

Probability of Target Impact (1–3 pts, A.T. context)

1 · Occas.
Low-use side/relocation trails; boundary lines and non-congregation corridor segments; dispersed/undesignated areas with limited public use.
2 · Interm.
Moderate-use campsites, vistas, or trail segments; secondary access roads and parking adjacent to moderate-use sites.
3 · Frequent
Shelters, designated overnight sites, privies, trailheads, bridges, high-use vistas, parking adjacent to high-use sites, and any area of congregation per ATC guidance.

ATC Hazard Tree Decision Workflow

Step 1
Trained personnel complete assessment (every 5 yrs, or after storms, reports, or forest health changes).
Step 2
No hazard → monitoring. Defect confirmed but below threshold → monitor at a frequency matched to severity. Hazard confirmed (imminent risk to a congregation area/structure) → recommend management action.
Step 3
Land manager reviews: declines (may recommend closure/further assessment) or approves removal.
Step 4–6
If removal can't happen immediately, land manager may direct site quarantine: flagging, signage, trailhead notices, online updates.
Step 7–9
Qualified personnel remove the tree per team requirements; reopen site if closed; resume monitoring.

Removal Team Requirements

Team
Minimum of two people per removal.
First aid
At least two team members trained in first aid.
Sawyer
At least one B- or C-Feller-certified sawyer required; sawyers don't exceed their comfort/certification level.
Rigging
If rigging is required, at least one member trained in tree risk assessment and arborist rigging must be present.

Bat Conservation Parameters (APPA Programmatic BA)

Threshold
Suitable NLEB/bat roost trees: live trees and snags ≥ 3" DBH with cavities, cracks, or exfoliating/loose bark.
Safe window
Nov 1 – Mar 31 (bat inactive season). Removal outside this window requires deferral or the imminent hazard exception.
Inspection
A roost inspection must be completed within 72 hrs prior to removal for any suitable roost tree removed outside the safe window.
Exception
A suitable roost tree posing an imminent hazard may be removed outside the window without waiting, if documented and, where applicable, USFWS/resource staff are consulted.
